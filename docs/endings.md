# ENDINGS & KINGSHIP — Blueprint

**Status:** Blueprint v1 (2026-09-24) · **Four endings, each siding with a faction the player encounters, the fourth of them hidden behind a very specific sequence of events** — the notes' own structure, adopted as written. The founding and rank systems beneath them are imported mechanics ([mechanics.md](mechanics.md)); the ending content is 100% canon.

## 1. The rules for endings (carried, and where they came from)

1. **The clock doesn't bend.** Canon: *"the empire lies on its last pillars before the great reset of the world wipes their land from the face of the earth"* (world-bible §4.1) — and every ending below has the Empire already broken or breaking. The endings decide what rises from the breaking, never whether it happens.
2. **All four endings are the notes' endings**, transcribed in §3 as canon — nothing added.
3. **Each ending has a price.** Every ending below carries its own dark consequence in the canon itself.
4. **The deed-recap (epitaph)** (§4) is an imported end-of-game system the notes do not mention; it renders whatever the player actually did.

## 2. Kingship: the player's own city-state `[CANON]` + parent mechanic

The notes: *"In the game the player can make his own city state, do business and forge alliances"*, and the end story is the player *"becoming his king by establishing his own traditional kingdom."* The parent mechanic for this already exists in full (parent endings §2): founding a polity in the ruins, rising to **rank 6: King** (rpg-systems §2.4), with the five requirements — people, food security, walls and a defence force, **a temple and a patron god** with the founding rite, recognition (treaty or open independence), and a royal seal — all carried unchanged ([mechanics.md](mechanics.md)).

- The notes name the player's claim **"the right of kingship"** `[CANON]`.
- **Where it can be founded:** `OPEN` for endings 1–3 (§11.13). **Canon for the hidden ending:** *"a new city on the ruins of Ur, with the foundations razed of the old City of Jewels"* (§3.4).
- **Ruling** (law table, land grants, taxes, cases, marriages) is the parent system, carried; its content (law table sources, customs) is `OPEN` ([game-design.md §11.15](game-design.md)).

## 3. The four endings `[CANON]`

### 3.1 Ending 1: The Empire
> *"If the player character decides to accept their past life as their new identity and become the emperor of the empire they founded and supplant their descendant, then the empire shall uphold the worship of Ishtar and seek to spread it everywhere they can and conquer every land they can, continuing the empire's life and eventually integrating the barbarians into the culture of the Empire. The drought shall be handled through deals with Ishtar — asking much of its people in avoiding catastrophe."*

- **The past life made good:** the player accepts his identity as the Empire's founder returned (the Law Giver of the City of Jewels, per the notes' implication — [game-design.md §3](game-design.md)) and supplants his own descendant on the throne.
- **The price (in the canon itself):** the worship of the deity the Brotherhood calls demonic becomes the empire's business forever, and the drought is staved off by **deals with Ishtar that ask much of the people**.
- **Unlock:** `OPEN` (§11.13) — parent pattern: standing with the Empire through the acts, plus the Act IV action.

### 3.2 Ending 2: The Sumerian Ending
> *"If the player character decides to support the Prophet in his quest to rebuild the Sumerian Empire and create a rebirth of Sumerian culture, they will create a secession and put Akkad in ashes, kick out all Akkadians and Barbarians and seek to establish rule by eradicating all Akkadians and Barbarians. The old classics of Sumerian culture shall be spread and shared, and the Sumerians will build an empire covering all of the valley. However, in so doing the cult of Ishtar shall be torn down and eradicated — but in doing so a new hidden evil will take its place: an ancient contemporary of Ishtar that will rule from behind the scenes, as the empire of the bull remains and its cult spreads instead…"*

- **The price (in the canon itself):** the eradication, and the **hidden evil** — tearing down the demonic deity's cult does not rid the valley of demons; it clears the throne-room for an older one. **The "empire of the bull"** is canon; what it is, is `OPEN` (§11.11).
- **Unlock:** `OPEN` (§11.13).

### 3.3 Ending 3: The Barbarian Ending
> *"If the player character decides to abandon the river lands in search of aiding these mysterious Barbarians, these foreigners shall sweep down the river lands like a horde of locusts, subjugating the inhabitants and creating their own empires — thus making the legacy of the Law Maker and the Sumerians finished. The poor administration of the Barbarians will lead to a decay of the land, and the eventual collapse of many traces of their history and culture; the economy and history shall fall into rot and be forgotten. The Barbarians shall rule with an iron and tribal fist, resisting integration, bringing with them their new customs of tribal warfare and the old shamanistic way of life which was part of Sumerian life before the urbanisation. The economy shall stagnate and the farm animals shall run free. There is no single true king that leads — for they are, and always will be, a wild bunch: even gathered under one banner they fall into bickering again and again. There is only the strong, and the council of warlords. There is only the gathering of strong individuals…"*

- **The price (in the canon itself):** the valley's memory itself rots — the Law Giver's and Sumer's legacies end, the land decays, the economy stagnates. No crown exists to wear: the player's place is in the **council of warlords**, among the strong.
- **Unlock:** `OPEN` (§11.13).

### 3.4 Ending 4 (hidden): The Brotherhood of the Serpent
> *"If the player character chooses to ally themselves with the Serpent Lords, they will give the lands ultimatum to none except a planned fall of the Empire, the rise of the council of warlords, and the eventual rise and return to the Sumerian empire — for a time only. The Barbarians shall be pushed into the Lost City of Jewels, so that it may be rid of contaminating and spreading the popularity of the demonic deity, albeit hard, since its roots have been planted deep. The Prophet of Ur shall try to establish a final desperate rebellion against the invading forces, and from there the player character shall call upon the right of kingship: as they made the City of Jewels, so too shall they make a new city on the ruins of Ur, with the foundations razed of the old City of Jewels. The Prophet shall try to resist the invasion of Barbarians and Empirials into his city and desperately summon his master into the world. At last he shall be defeated by the player character and a priest of the BotS. Then one final conversation shall happen between them, as the player character tells the priest the new age shall be one of war and passion — seeing to rid the world of evils and desperately fight — but ultimately, unknown to the priest and player, these passions will manifest themselves into fighting the inner battle instead, and the age of empires shall take off to heights unknown, and new nations shall try to imitate what was in the past of war, and from there grip man even further into war and pain, further leading down to chaos. But from this chaos new glory shall arise, so someday the sun may rise as it went down once again…"*

- **The shape:** the Brotherhood's planned sequence of ages — Empire falls, warlords rise, Sumer returns for a time; the Barbarians pushed into the Lost City of Jewels to scour the demonic deity's roots from it; the Prophet's last rebellion and his **summoning of his master** (the parent's **divine intervention** family is exactly this mechanic's shape: a world event, not a spell — rpg-systems §10.3); the player and a Brotherhood priest defeat him; the player calls the **right of kingship** and founds his city on the ruins of Ur; the final conversation — and the ironies the player and priest never see.
- **The price (in the canon itself):** the age they open becomes an age of war and passion that grips man deeper into chaos — the new glory is real, and far off. And the Brotherhood's gift costs the player what it always costs: he is **needed elsewhere** (§5).
- **Access:** the notes: hidden, *"only accessible after doing a very specific sequence of events"* — the steps are `OPEN` (§11.12). The mechanic is the parent's silent-sequence pattern: no quest markers, no hints beyond one rumour; miss a step and the path closes without ever announcing it existed (parent endings §3.4). Worthy-only access is also canon in the Brotherhood's nature (world-bible §2).

## 4. The deed-recap (epitaph) — an imported system

Each ending closes with the imported end-of-game **deed-recap** system (the "reputation epitaph", parent endings §4): it renders what the player actually did — and, if the heir decision is yes ([game-design.md §11.4](game-design.md)), the whole line. **The notes don't mention it; it carries as a mechanic only**, and its content is drawn from canon roles: *emperor returned*, *founder of the traditional kingdom*, *the one who broke the Empire*, *warlord of the council*.

## 5. The cliffhanger frame `[CANON]`

The notes' headline arc (world-bible; [game-design.md §1](game-design.md)): at the end, the player has severely weakened the Empire and the worship of its deity, **works closely with the Brotherhood in making his own nation** — and the Brotherhood tells him **he is needed elsewhere**: they will **initiate him into their secrets, continuing his service to the gods and eventually cleansing his sins**. The end story is *"a cliffhanger of the player going out and becoming his king by establishing his own traditional kingdom to counteract these degenerate forces."*

`OPEN` (§11.14 of game-design): whether this frame is **Ending 4's epilogue** (its content matches it — the Brotherhood alliance, the founding of the kingdom), a post-ending scene shown on **all** paths, or the canon continuation hook for a future game. It is transcribed here once, as canon, and placed by the designer's decision.

The Brotherhood's promise — *initiate him into their secrets, continuing his service to the gods and eventually cleansing his sins* — is the imported **Purity** condition's arc told at story level (game-design §8.1): the man who arrives in chains carries defilement; the order offers its cleansing. And his kingdom exists to *"counteract these degenerate forces"* — the notes' own name for what the Empire's worship has become.

## 6. Voice (carried)

- **The player character is optionally voiced** — a setting switches the player's voice on or off (the notes' first line; parent game-design §13.5). Carried unchanged.
- **NPC voices:** `OPEN` — subtitles plus short spoken exclamations (the parent's budget default), or voiced main NPCs (parent endings §7.1).

## 7. What this doc does not do

It does not write the unlock sequences (§11.12–13), the ending quests, or the "empire of the bull". The notes don't give them, the parent mechanics don't contain them, and nothing here is invented to fill the silence.
