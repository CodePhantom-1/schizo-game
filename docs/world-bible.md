## 1. Cosmology: the ages and the flood `[CANON]`

- Legends speak of a time when the Sumerian people migrated into their northern home **with their boats from a rich undying land in the south**.
- Once there were **five great city states of Sumer** living in a golden age. That age ended when **the lord of water and wisdom saw that men had become unworthy to live in it**, ended the age, and came **the flood**, which wiped away the old world and left the current one to men.
- The world is now **on the verge of entering another age, as it has many times before** — one more violent and chaotic than the last, that will demand men **balance compassion with wrath, justice and petty revenge**.
- These tales shall be forgotten over time, but **one of many orders of men has made it their duty to preserve this old knowledge and rectify mankind back to glory once more**.
- The collapse now beginning is **"the great reset of the world"**: the Empire "lies on its last pillars before the great reset of the world wipes their land from the face of the earth."
- The notes name the coming age once: **the War Age** — *"the War Age's dawn has shown its claw marks on this land"* (the South, §7). The hidden ending's final conversation names its character: *"the new age shall be one of war and passion"* ([endings.md §3.4](endings.md)).

## 2. The Brotherhood of the Serpent (origin and nature) `[CANON]`

- The Brotherhood's origins date back to the **Ante-Diluvian times**: a **priestly secret order hidden from the public, like white light, but active in the affairs of men**.
- It exists **to preserve balance and uphold the natural law**.
- With the corruption of kings, the reshaping of the world and the decay of virtue, the Brotherhood seeks to re-establish order **by working in the background**.
- It **can only be met by those worthy enough to stand face to face with the forces which have driven creation since the beginning, and behold the destiny of men**.
- Legends it keeps: before the flood, the **lord of water and wisdom** ended the golden age of the five first cities (§1). The Empire sent expeditions south to reclaim and rediscover the rich undying land of the migration legends (§4.4).

## 3. The origin myth of the lands of Urash `[CANON]`

- In the beginning the lands of **Urash** were home to **nomads and a few farmers** who harvested wheat and barley and worshipped their **earth deities**. They lived simply, without much law.
- Then, from the coasts of the south, came **an armada of boats**. These foreigners founded the **first great city**, a city of laws with a numerous people, and raised the first great temple to their deities of **salt and fresh water**, offering them sacrifices of **fish and sea gems**. They moved inland, settled other communities, mixed with locals, and spread the worship of water, creating mixed new deities.
- Then, from the north, came **new settlers who subdued the former rulers** and became the new elite. Before them there had been **only priests** ruling their communities; the northerners **"lowered kingship"** upon the land — the first city they made was the **City of the Sky Father**, and shortly after they lowered kingship upon the **City of the Abyss**.
- Under northern rule the City of the Abyss **shifted from the worship of the two waters to the Water Lord and his consort the Swamp Queen**.
- The natives' earth worship met the foreigners' sky worship. The sky god's **two sons**: one came to rule the **City of the Abyss** (also called the **city of Mystics**), the other became ruler of the **City of Kings** — the place where the greatest kings are laid in **massive underground catacombs** to join the ancestors in the **Kingdom of the Dead**.

`OPEN`: the notes call the game's land "a strange land ruled by two ancient peoples" (§6, the opening). Whether these are the two peoples of this myth (southern boat folk, northern settlers) is for the designer to confirm.

## 4. The four powers `[CANON]`

Full faction mechanics in [game-design.md §5](game-design.md). This section is their lore, as written.

### 4.1 The Empire
- For almost **180 years** the great Empire has stretched across the lands of the **Taurissian people**, conquering other lands **for tribute and gold**, in honor of its **patron deity, the lady of the skies**.
- The Taurissians were once **simple farmer communities** that grew into **influential city states** with great effect on their neighbours, then turned to warfare when **men's lust for gold became too much**, accelerating conquest more than ever.
- The Empire that shone the most was **the Empire made by the great Law Giver of the lost city of jewels**. For many generations **the dynasty of this empire has held sway — but that hold is about to break**.
- With **the drought that is breaking the world**, many **foreigners from the east** have begun to raid the cities and migrate into the Empire in greater numbers, pillaging. **The south** sees the perfect moment to strike and **tear up the empire with patriotism and liberty**.
- The Empire conquered the city states of Sumer **from the north** and **replaced their former kings with rulers of its own** (§4.2).

### 4.2 The Neo-Sumerian Rebellion
- The city states of **Sumer** were once farming communities that became **trading hubs, then rich city states** which sought to conquer each other mainly through trade and business. As men's lust for bounty grew they adopted new ways of searching for gold and treasures; the first empires arose within Sumer, city states allying and conquering one another.
- All of it ended when **the Empire of the north conquered them all** and replaced their kings.
- Now, with the weakening of the Empire, **a seer from the city of Ur** — the **mysterious sage of the swamps** — seeks to **unite the city states, push back the invading barbarians, fix the drought and the shattering of the land, and usher in a new age of bounty and a rebirth of Sumerian culture**.

The notes describe the south's leader twice — here as *"a mysterious sage of the swamps"*, and as *"the enigmatic Prophet"* of the City of the Moon (§5) — both leading the same southern rebellion; the blueprint treats them as the same man.

### 4.3 The Barbarians
- In the **east** lies a great range of mountains, home to valleys and plains, and within it a **mysterious land of foreign people with foreign customs**, many of them **cattle nomads**.
- With the breaking of the world their life has shifted more than ever: **from the nomadic cattle life to active raids on the Empire**. A **new form of thought** has gripped these people and turned them into a **warrior people**.
- They are **tribal and scattered**, bound loosely by one goal: **survival of the breaking, by finding new lands in the west**.

### 4.4 The Brotherhood of the Serpent (the order in the world today)
- The Empire sought to reclaim and rediscover the rich undying land of the migration legends by **sending expeditions south**.
- The order preserves the tales of the flood, the five first cities and the ages of the world (§1–2), **working in the background of the affairs of men**.
- Its members describe the Empire's patron deity as a **demonic entity** ([game-design.md §3](game-design.md)); its aim in the coming collapse is set out in [endings.md §3.4](endings.md).

## 8. Magick `[CANON]` (practices; `[A]` where real)

The forms the Akkadians and Sumerians of this world "shall be versed in", as the notes list them. How each maps onto the existing magic system is in [game-design.md §8](game-design.md); the forms **without an existing mechanic** are opt-in decisions, not designs.

### 8.1 The forms of magick
- **Numerology** — basic or advanced.
- **A really primitive form of kabbalah and/or hermeticism — "as above, so below."**
- **Astrology** — definitely basic forms: all 12 zodiacs, but in a primitive form; add some extra constellations, but not all.
- **Divination** — haruspicy, very common in Mesopotamia, called **Barûtu** `[A]`.
- **Sex magick** — tied in the notes to Inanna.
- **Hymns** — vibrating the names of deities.
- **Astrotheology** — the planets recognised in the pantheon: **Sun, Moon, Saturn and Ishtar**, as well as Mercury-ish — **Enki was sometimes identified with Mercury** `[A]`.
- **Demonology** — the people recognise a number of demons, some evil, some neutral, some benevolent; **much of medicine consists of exorcisms** `[A]`.
- **Amulets / Talismans** — worn to repel demons and bad spirits `[A]`.
- **Exorcisms / Zisurru** — protecting people and places, with different types of flour, followed by the gloss **SAG.BA.SAG.BA** `[A]`.

### 8.2 The forms of divination `[A]`
- **Astragalomancy** — roll dice or other items to read what the future holds; see also **cleromancy**.
- **Pessomancy** — as astragalomancy, but with coloured items instead of numbered ones.
- **Haruspicy** — the liver of a burned goat predicts the future (Barûtu, §8.1).
- **Aeromancy** — cloud formations, wind currents and cosmological events such as comets. Sub-types in the notes: **austromancy** (wind), **ceraunoscopy** (thunder and lightning), **chaomancy** (aerial vision), **meteormancy** (meteors and shooting stars), **nephomancy** (clouds).

### 8.3 The planetary magic lists `[CANON]`

- **Earth:** physical wealth; physical manifestation of things (i.e. superior crop yield) — nature magic.
- **Moon:** divination — psychological magic — reading the future — dreams — fortune telling — animal telepathy — control minds (*the notes' own warning: "don't do it, it is bad karma"*) — astral travel — telepathy — psychometry.
- **Mercury:** all forms of high/ceremonial rituals — invocations — pathworking.
- **Venus** *(the notes end here; this entry was written at the designer's explicit commission, 2026-09-24, to complete the list — everything above is verbatim):* love and desire - lust and attraction - **sex magick (the sacred rites of Inanna)** - fertility of people, herds and fields - beauty and adornment - allure - the binding of hearts (*binding against the will is bad karma, as with the Moon's mind-control*) - unions: marriages and alliances - **the morning star: battle-fury, victory, conquest and tribute — the lady of the skies' own road** - the evening star: mercy, peace sworn after war -

The Venus entry is **Ishtar's portfolio** — Venus is Ishtar's planet (§8.1), and Ishtar is the deity the entire narrative orbits: the Empire's patron, the Brotherhood's "demonic entity", the cult the endings raise or eradicate. The dual aspect is hers in the notes' own material: the Empire is *"built on the principle of tribute and conquest"* (morning star) and the player *"forge[s] alliances"* under her evening-star face.

The lists the notes never wrote: **Sun** and **Saturn** (both are recognised planets in §8.1's astrotheology) — `OPEN` whether the designer writes them. The four completed lists are canon data; converting them into mechanics is the opt-in decision in [game-design.md §8.3](game-design.md).

## 9. The wider world `[CANON]`

The notes' header is explicit: *"Requirements for designing the world (NOT THE GAME MAP BUT THE WHOLE WORLD ITSELF)"* — this is world lore, separate from the game map. Transcribed as written:

- An **American continent**, with a history of having been settled by the **Atlanteans**, named after a **plumed serpent** after the very mystery traditions; home to ruins of an ancient people.
- The **Giza pyramids** — home of ancient magics.
- The **Middle East** — centre of life, cradle of civilisation, connecting three continents.
- **Europe** — home of the white race; the cradle of civilisation lies near it.
- An **India-inspired region** east of the cradle of civilisation — centre of wisdom.

## 11. How canon is tagged (policy)

1. `[CANON]` — from the designer's notes, transcribed. Copy-editing touched spelling and grammar only, never content.
2. `[A]` — material the notes import from the real Sumerian/Akkadian record (pantheon names, the city lists, Barûtu, zisurru, astragalomancy and the other divination forms). These rows get real sources in the content database, exactly as the parent project sources its material.
3. `INVENTED: EFFECT` — carried unchanged from the parent magic policy (rpg-systems §0): the practice may be canon or `[A]`; that it *works* is the game's world, flagged in the codex.
4. `OPEN` — a gap, ambiguity or contradiction in the notes. Routed to [game-design.md §11](game-design.md). **Never resolved silently.**

## 12. Designer rulings on open canon (D-013 → D-016) `[CANON]`

Three gaps the content storm surfaced were ruled by the designer on 2026-09-25:

1. **Sea gems** (the notes name them only as offering material, notes L57): they are **gifts from the abyssal water god Enki — an extension of his power. They are magical gems, controlled by the god of magic, Enki.** Physically they are gems of the sea that can be found on the shore; they come in **platonic shapes** and out of **sea materials like coral or seaweed**. (Consequence already standing in the data: the first great temple's offering of fish and sea gems to the two waters returns the water god's own gifts to the waters; Enki is now named the god of magic — of a piece with his Mercury face in the astrotheology, notes L195.)
2. **The regalia** (the notes say the northerners "lowered kingship" but never name the objects, notes L58): **the emperor wears no regalia — naked except underwear.** There are no regalia objects; the nakedness is the statement. (The OPEN items row is resolved by negation and removed; a customs row records it.)
3. **The tribute basket** (conquest "for tribute and gold", notes L15;L104): **gems and fruits and a bottle of a strange white liquid.** The liquid's nature stays deliberately unwritten — the basket's mystery travels with the tribute.
